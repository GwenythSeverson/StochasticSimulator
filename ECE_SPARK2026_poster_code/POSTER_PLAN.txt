================================================================================================
ECE SPARK 2026 -- FAULT TOLERANCE POSTER PLAN
================================================================================================

Everything below is measured. Four units, one operand pair each, N = 256, exhaustive fault
enumeration verified against random trials. No orbit, no upset rate, no environment anywhere --
the x axis is BITS FLIPPED throughout, which is a circuit property and holds whatever caused
them. Applying a LEO or GEO rate is a separate, later step.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE THESIS -- ONE SENTENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    UNARY REDUNDANCY IS WHAT BUYS FAULT TOLERANCE. uGEMM's units buy accuracy by compressing
    state into a binary register -- and whether that costs you depends entirely on WHAT THE
    REGISTER HOLDS.

        uMUL's  8-bit register holds an OPERAND   ->  one strike can zero the answer, permanently
        uSADD's 1-bit accumulator holds a RESIDUE ->  one strike costs at most 0.0039, ever --
                                                      the same as one ordinary stream bit

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE MEASURED NUMBERS BEHIND IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MULTIPLICATION      0.5 x 0.5 = 0.25, N = 256
                                        AND gate            uMUL
    operand surface                     512 bits            264 bits (256 stream + 8 register)
    decorrelation filter needed?        YES                 no
    reach at 1 flip                     0.246 - 0.254       0.000 - 0.375
    reach at 36 flips                   0.109 - 0.391       0.000 - 0.613
    worst damage from ONE strike        0.0039  (1/256)     0.2500  (MSB of the register)
    distribution shape                  unimodal, -> normal bimodal, never normal
    variance carried by 3% of trials    n/a                 95.5%
    mean                                0.25 at every f     0.25 at every f

ADDITION            (0.5 + 0.5)/2 = 0.5, N = 256
                                        MUX adder           uSADD
    operand surface                     768 bits (a+b+sel)  512 bits (a+b) + 3 bits of state
    of which LIVE                       384 of 768          512 of 512
    decorrelation filter needed?        YES                 no
    full reach at 32 flips              0.375 - 0.625       0.438 - 0.563   <- HALF
    middle 90% at 32 flips              0.477 - 0.523       0.484 - 0.516
    worst damage from ONE state strike  no state to strike  0.0039  (= one stream bit)
    healthy RMSE vs exact               0.0174              0.000000

    THE ADDITION RESULT IS A CLEAN SWEEP and it surprised me: uSADD wins on accuracy AND on
    fault tolerance AND on surface area. Its worst-case reach is HALF the MUX's because the 1/2
    scaling attenuates input faults before they reach the output -- 32 flips move the credit by
    32 but the answer by only 16 bits. The MUX has no such attenuation: every live flip is worth
    a full output bit.

    THE MULTIPLICATION RESULT GOES THE OTHER WAY. uMUL is more accurate than the AND gate
    (no correlation error, no warm-up debt) and strictly worse under fault.

3. THE FIGURES ON THE POSTER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FIVE figures. Two rows of two for the comparison, one centrepiece that carries the thesis.

    ROW 1 -- MULTIPLICATION: THE TRADE GOES BADLY
      [1]  Multiplication/AND_mul/AND_Mul_poster_faults.png
           The AND gate. Symmetric, unimodal, graceful: 512 operand bits, every one worth the
           same 1/256, so 36 flips move the answer by at most 0.14 and the middle 90% stays
           inside +/- 0.03. Nothing is special, nothing is catastrophic.

      [2]  Multiplication/uMUL_mul/uMUL_poster_faults_damaged.png
           uMUL's REGISTER-ONLY campaign. Reaches 0.000 from ONE flip. Put it directly beside [1]
           and the contrast needs no caption.

           NOTE THIS IS NO LONGER A CONDITIONAL PLOT. It used to be "what happens GIVEN a
           register bit was hit", drawn from the same sweep as its companion. It is now its own
           experiment -- flips land only on the 8 register bits, 180,988 real gate simulations of
           its own -- and its x axis is LINEAR and stops at 8, because the register has 8 bits.
           Do not describe it as a slice or a percentage of anything.

           STILL SAY THE FREQUENCY OUT LOUD, because the separate campaigns no longer state it
           for you. Of a single upset landing anywhere in uMUL's 264 bits of operand storage,
           8/264 = 3% hits the register. Its companion uMUL_poster_faults_intact.png is the other
           97% and it looks like [1]. The honest and more interesting claim is the pair: uMUL
           fails RARELY and CATASTROPHICALLY, the AND gate fails CONSTANTLY and NEGLIGIBLY.

           ONE COUNTERINTUITIVE POINT WORTH A SENTENCE: on [2] the damage is WORST AT ONE FLIP.
           The floor rises after that and at 8 flips the answer is back at exactly 0.25 -- 128
           XOR 255 = 127, which still reads 64/256. For this register, one upset is the dangerous
           number, which is the opposite of what a fault model usually assumes.

    ROW 2 -- ADDITION: THE TRADE GOES WELL
      [3]  Addition/MUX_add/MUX_Add_poster_faults.png
           The MUX adder over all 768 bits -- INCLUDING THE SELECT STREAM, which is the thing
           people forget is a fault surface. +/- 0.125 at 32 flips.

      [4]  Addition/uSADD_add/uSADD_Add_poster_faults.png
           uSADD on the same axes. +/- 0.0625 at 32 flips: half the reach, two thirds the
           surface, and no select stream to protect.

    CENTREPIECE -- WHY
      [5]  Multiplication/uMUL_mul/uMUL_poster_register.png
           uMUL's 8-bit operand register expanded: the SIZE of each bit's single-flip damage,
           against one stream bit's 1/256 as a dashed reference. A clean geometric ladder from 1x
           to 64x, doubling every bit.

           THIS PANEL IS ABSOLUTE VALUE, DELIBERATELY, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT THE CENTREPIECE.
           |delta| = (2^b / 2^width) * p0, which depends on the BIT and not on what the register
           holds. Only the SIGN depends on the operand -- bit clear means the flip sets it and the
           answer rises, bit set means the flip clears it and the answer falls. So you can stand
           at the poster and say "one upset in the top register bit does what 64 stream upsets do"
           for ANY number the register might hold, not just 0.5.

           THE SIGNED VERSION WAS ABOUT 128, NOT ABOUT uMUL. It showed seven bars up and one down,
           and that shape is an accident of 128 being one-hot -- at value 64 it would be bit 6
           pointing down, at 255 every bar would point down. Absolute value removes the example
           and leaves the architecture.

           IF SOMEONE ASKS "so which bit takes it to zero?", that is figure [2]'s floor, not this
           panel's job. For value = 128 it is bit 7, because 128 has exactly one bit set so
           clearing it clears the whole operand. Say it as the example it is.

           THE BOTTOM TWO BARS BOTH READ 1x, and that is the output quantising, not a broken
           ladder: the answer is a count out of 256 and cannot move by less than 1/256, so bit 0's
           ideal 0.002 floors up. Below bit 1 the register is finer than the answer can express.

           SAY THE uSADD HALF OUT LOUD RATHER THAN PLOTTING IT. uSADD also puts state in a
           binary register, and the worst ONE strike on any of its three state bits (1-bit
           accumulator + 2-bit PC bus) is 0.0039 -- exactly what one ordinary stream bit is
           worth, and 64x smaller than uMUL's. That is a sentence, not a shape, so it belongs in
           the caption rather than in a bar chart. The measured per-bit, per-cycle data is in
           Addition/uSADD_add/uSADD_Add_poster_state.csv if anyone asks to see it.

           If you only have room for THREE figures, use [1], [2] and [5].

6. WHAT NOT TO CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Do not attach an orbit or an upset rate to any of this. None of the figures contain one, on
    purpose. "1 in N operations in LEO" is a separate study.
  - Do not call the high-f columns a predicted operating point. They are a stress sweep that maps
    the response function; f = 1 is the column any realistic rate exercises.
  - Do not claim uMUL is worse overall. It is MORE accurate when healthy -- that is why it
    exists -- and the poster's point is that the accuracy is bought with redundancy, not that
    the unit is bad.
  - Do not present the uSADD PC/accumulator sweep as layout-exhaustive. It is site-exhaustive:
    every bit and strike timing is measured, but multi-strike layouts over that state do not
    collapse and were not enumerated. The .cpp header says so and so should you.
