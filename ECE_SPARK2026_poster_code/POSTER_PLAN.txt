================================================================================================
ECE SPARK 2026 -- FAULT TOLERANCE POSTER PLAN
Which figures to show, in what order, and what each one is for.
================================================================================================

Everything below is measured. Four units, one operand pair each, N = 256, exhaustive fault
enumeration verified against random trials. No orbit, no upset rate, no environment anywhere --
the x axis is BITS FLIPPED throughout, which is a circuit property and holds whatever caused
them. Applying a LEO or GEO rate is a separate, later step.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE THESIS -- ONE SENTENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    UNARY REDUNDANCY IS WHAT BUYS FAULT TOLERANCE. uGEMM's units buy accuracy by compressing
    state into a binary register -- and whether that costs you depends entirely on WHAT THE
    REGISTER HOLDS.

        uMUL's  8-bit register holds an OPERAND   ->  one strike can zero the answer, permanently
        uSADD's 1-bit accumulator holds a RESIDUE ->  one strike costs at most 0.0039, ever --
                                                      the same as one ordinary stream bit

    Same idea, opposite consequence. That asymmetry is the poster, and it is the thing nobody
    gets from an accuracy table.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE MEASURED NUMBERS BEHIND IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MULTIPLICATION      0.5 x 0.5 = 0.25, N = 256
                                        AND gate            uMUL
    operand surface                     512 bits            264 bits (256 stream + 8 register)
    decorrelation filter needed?        YES                 no
    reach at 1 flip                     0.246 - 0.254       0.000 - 0.375
    reach at 36 flips                   0.109 - 0.391       0.000 - 0.613
    worst damage from ONE strike        0.0039  (1/256)     0.2500  (MSB of the register)
    distribution shape                  unimodal, -> normal bimodal, never normal
    variance carried by 3% of trials    n/a                 95.5%
    mean                                0.25 at every f     0.25 at every f

ADDITION            (0.5 + 0.5)/2 = 0.5, N = 256
                                        MUX adder           uSADD
    operand surface                     768 bits (a+b+sel)  512 bits (a+b) + 3 bits of state
    of which LIVE                       384 of 768          512 of 512
    decorrelation filter needed?        YES                 no
    full reach at 32 flips              0.375 - 0.625       0.438 - 0.563   <- HALF
    middle 90% at 32 flips              0.477 - 0.523       0.484 - 0.516
    worst damage from ONE state strike  no state to strike  0.0039  (= one stream bit)
    healthy RMSE vs exact               0.0174              0.000000

    THE ADDITION RESULT IS A CLEAN SWEEP and it surprised me: uSADD wins on accuracy AND on
    fault tolerance AND on surface area. Its worst-case reach is HALF the MUX's because the 1/2
    scaling attenuates input faults before they reach the output -- 32 flips move the credit by
    32 but the answer by only 16 bits. The MUX has no such attenuation: every live flip is worth
    a full output bit.

    THE MULTIPLICATION RESULT GOES THE OTHER WAY. uMUL is more accurate than the AND gate
    (no correlation error, no warm-up debt) and strictly worse under fault.

3. THE FIGURES TO PUT ON THE POSTER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FIVE figures. Two rows of two for the comparison, one centrepiece that carries the thesis.

    ROW 1 -- MULTIPLICATION: THE TRADE GOES BADLY
      [1]  Multiplication/AND_mul/AND_Mul_poster_faults.png
           The AND gate. Symmetric, unimodal, graceful: 512 operand bits, every one worth the
           same 1/256, so 36 flips move the answer by at most 0.14 and the middle 90% stays
           inside +/- 0.03. Nothing is special, nothing is catastrophic.

      [2]  Multiplication/uMUL_mul/uMUL_poster_faults_damaged.png
           uMUL when its register is struck. Reaches 0.000 from ONE flip and stays reachable
           there at every level. Put it directly beside [1] on the same page and the contrast
           needs no caption.

           SAY OUT LOUD that this is the conditional plot: it is what happens GIVEN a register
           bit was hit, which is 3% of single upsets. Its companion
           uMUL_poster_faults_intact.png is the other 97% and it looks like [1]. Showing only
           the damaged half without saying so would be cherry-picking; showing both halves and
           the 3% is the honest and more interesting claim -- uMUL fails RARELY and
           CATASTROPHICALLY, the AND gate fails CONSTANTLY and NEGLIGIBLY.

    ROW 2 -- ADDITION: THE TRADE GOES WELL
      [3]  Addition/MUX_add/MUX_Add_poster_faults.png
           The MUX adder over all 768 bits -- INCLUDING THE SELECT STREAM, which is the thing
           people forget is a fault surface. +/- 0.125 at 32 flips.

      [4]  Addition/uSADD_add/uSADD_Add_poster_faults.png
           uSADD on the same axes. +/- 0.0625 at 32 flips: half the reach, two thirds the
           surface, and no select stream to protect.

    CENTREPIECE -- WHY
      [5]  Multiplication/uMUL_mul/uMUL_poster_register.png
           uMUL's 8-bit operand register expanded: each bit's single-flip damage against the
           stream bit's +/- 1/256 drawn as a dashed reference. Seven bits push the answer up by
           geometrically growing amounts; bit 7 takes it to a hard zero. ONE upset there is worth
           64 stream upsets, and the panel shows exactly why: value = 128 has one bit set, so
           clearing it clears the operand.

           SAY THE uSADD HALF OUT LOUD RATHER THAN PLOTTING IT. uSADD also puts state in a
           binary register, and the worst ONE strike on any of its three state bits (1-bit
           accumulator + 2-bit PC bus) is 0.0039 -- exactly what one ordinary stream bit is
           worth, and 64x smaller than uMUL's. That is a sentence, not a shape, so it belongs in
           the caption rather than in a bar chart. The measured per-bit, per-cycle data is in
           Addition/uSADD_add/uSADD_Add_poster_state.csv if anyone asks to see it.

           If you only have room for THREE figures, use [1], [2] and [5].

4. HOW TO COMPARE THEM FAIRLY -- READ THIS BEFORE MOUNTING ANYTHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  SAME X AXIS, DIFFERENT SURFACE SIZES. All four figures use "bits flipped" on the same log
  ticks, but the surfaces differ: 512 (AND), 264 (uMUL), 768 (MUX), 512 (uSADD). f = 8 is
  therefore a heavier dose for uMUL than for the MUX. State the surface size on each figure --
  all four already do, in the x-axis label -- and do NOT let a reader infer that equal f means
  equal stress. The honest framing is "given f upsets landed here, this is what happens".

  CHECK THE VERTICAL SCALES. Two of them are shared on purpose and one pair is not:
      SHARED     [1] AND gate and uMUL's operand-INTACT figure both run 0.10 to 0.40, so they
                 can be mounted side by side and compared directly. That comparison is the
                 point of the pair, so do not retune one without the other.
      NOT SHARED uMUL's intact (0.10-0.40) against its damaged (0-0.70). On the damaged figure's
                 axis the intact panel would be a flat line, which is itself the result but shows
                 nothing. Both subtitles state their range.
      DIFFERENT  The multiplication figures centre on 0.25 and the addition figures on 0.5.
  Do not compare whisker heights across figures by eye without reading the axis first.

  THE MEAN IS PINNED AT THE TARGET IN THREE OF THE FOUR UNITS -- the AND gate, uMUL and the MUX
  adder all sit exactly on target at every flip level. Faults add variance, not drift. That makes
  the mean the UNinteresting quantity; the reach and the band are what to point at.

  uSADD IS THE EXCEPTION, AND ONLY AT ODD FLIP COUNTS. Its mean is exactly on 0.5 for even f and
  0.498047 for odd f -- low by exactly half an output bit. This is not a bug and not drift; it is
  the floor. uSADD emits floor(total credit / n), and an odd total leaves half a bit stranded in
  the accumulator as residue. At f = 1 that shows up starkly: the only reachable outcomes are 127
  and 128 ones, so a single flip can LOSE a bit or do nothing, but can never ADD one. Odd f
  always reaches one step further down than up.

  If a reviewer asks why the f = 1 whisker on the uSADD figure only goes downward, that is the
  answer, and it is the same residue that is the unit's only healthy error. The accumulator can
  withhold output but never manufacture it.

  FOR uMUL, DO NOT QUOTE A STANDARD DEVIATION AT ALL: 95% of its variance comes from 3% of its
  trials, so mean +/- sigma is wrong in both directions at once (see
  uMUL_mul/HOW_THIS_DATA_WAS_MADE.txt section 6c).

5. THE ONE-LINE CAPTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  [1]  "512 unary operand bits, every one worth 1/256. No bit is special."
  [2]  "264 operand bits -- but 8 of them are a binary register, and one strike takes the
        answer to zero. This is the 3% of upsets that land there."
  [3]  "768 bits, and a third of them are the select stream nobody counts."
  [4]  "512 bits, no select stream, and the 1/2 scaling halves what a fault can do."
       If asked about the one-flip whisker pointing only downward: uSADD emits floor(credit/2),
       the clean total 256 sits on a floor boundary, so one flip either LOSES a bit (255 -> 127)
       or gains nothing (257 -> 128). See section 4 and uSADD_add/HOW_THIS_DATA_WAS_MADE.txt.
  [5]  "Both units hold binary state. uMUL's holds an OPERAND -- one strike zeroes the`r`n        answer. uSADD's holds a RESIDUE -- one strike costs 1/256, like any other bit."

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT NOT TO CLAIM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Do not attach an orbit or an upset rate to any of this. None of the figures contain one, on
    purpose. "1 in N operations in LEO" is a separate study.
  - Do not call the high-f columns a predicted operating point. They are a stress sweep that maps
    the response function; f = 1 is the column any realistic rate exercises.
  - Do not claim uMUL is worse overall. It is MORE accurate when healthy -- that is why it
    exists -- and the poster's point is that the accuracy is bought with redundancy, not that
    the unit is bad.
  - Do not present the uSADD PC/accumulator sweep as layout-exhaustive. It is site-exhaustive:
    every bit and strike timing is measured, but multi-strike layouts over that state do not
    collapse and were not enumerated. The .cpp header says so and so should you.

7. REGENERATING EVERYTHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    stochastic_computer.exe --gtest_filter=AndMulPoster.*:uMULPoster.*:MuxAddPoster.*:uSADDAddPoster.*
    then run the four plot_*.m scripts in MATLAB.

  Each folder carries its own HOW_THIS_DATA_WAS_MADE.txt with the full method, the trial ledger,
  and the honest limitations. Read those before answering questions at the poster -- every number
  above is traceable to a measured gate run, and the reviewer who asks "how many trials is that
  actually?" should get the ledger, not a shrug.
